## Understanding Obstructive Sleep Apnea

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) occurs when the airway becomes blocked during sleep—typically by the soft tissues at the back of the throat. This blockage disrupts airflow, causes repeated pauses in breathing, and forces your body to wake frequently throughout the night, often without you realizing it.

Common symptoms include:

* ◆ Loud, chronic snoring
* ◆ Waking up gasping for air
* ◆ Daytime fatigue or irritability
* ◆ Morning headaches
* ◆ Poor concentration or memory loss
* ◆ Dry mouth or sore throat in the morning

These symptoms are often dismissed as ordinary tiredness or stress, which is part of why sleep apnea frequently goes undiagnosed for years. If a partner has mentioned your snoring or noticed you stop breathing during sleep, that observation is often the first real clue, and it’s worth taking seriously.

Left untreated, sleep apnea can lead to serious health risks including high blood pressure, heart disease, and metabolic disorders.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an oral appliance replace my CPAP machine?


For many patients, especially those with mild to moderate obstructive sleep apnea or anyone who is CPAP-intolerant, yes. A sleep study helps confirm whether an oral appliance is an appropriate primary treatment for your specific case.

### Is oral appliance therapy effective for all sleep apnea cases?


It is most effective for mild to moderate obstructive sleep apnea or for patients who are CPAP-intolerant. A sleep study will determine if you’re a good candidate.

### Is the appliance comfortable?


Yes. It’s custom designed to fit your mouth and support natural jaw positioning. Most patients adjust quickly and find it far more tolerable than CPAP.

### Will this stop my snoring?


In many cases, yes. By keeping the airway open, the oral appliance reduces or eliminates snoring caused by tissue vibration in the throat.

### How do I get started?


You’ll begin with a consultation and medical history review. If needed, we’ll coordinate with your physician for a sleep study before designing your custom appliance.

### Do I need a new sleep study if I already have one?


Not necessarily. If you’ve already been diagnosed with sleep apnea, that information is typically enough to begin evaluating you for oral appliance therapy.

### Is oral appliance therapy covered differently than CPAP?


Coverage varies by insurance plan and provider. Our team can help you understand your options during your consultation.
