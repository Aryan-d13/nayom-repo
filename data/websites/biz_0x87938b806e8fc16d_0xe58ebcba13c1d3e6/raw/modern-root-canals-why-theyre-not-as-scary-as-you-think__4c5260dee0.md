## When Root Canals Become Necessary

A tooth infection can begin quietly. While deep decay is a common cause, infections may also start from a crack, chip, or injury. Once bacteria reach the inner pulp of the tooth, the infection can progress rapidly and lead to significant pain.

Many people first notice:

* Pain when chewing
* Sensitivity to hot or cold that doesn’t go away
* Swelling around the gums or jaw

In these cases, a root canal is often the most effective way to eliminate infection, alleviate pain, and prevent the need for tooth removal.

## Addressing the Fear Around Root Canals

For years, root canals were thought to be one of the most painful dental procedures. In truth, the procedure itself is not painful, especially with today’s technology and local anesthesia. The pain that brings people to the dentist is caused by the infection, not the treatment.

Modern root canals are designed to be comfortable, and many patients feel a sense of relief once the source of their pain is removed. Recovery is typically smooth, with mild soreness that fades within a few days.

## What to Expect During the Process

The procedure usually takes place over two visits. The first appointment focuses on comfort and precision as the dentist numbs the area, isolates the tooth, and removes the infected tissue. The canals are then shaped and cleaned with special instruments, followed by placement of a temporary filling or crown.

During the second visit, the tooth is disinfected again if needed, then sealed with a durable filling material. A permanent crown may be added to restore full strength and function.

## Why It’s Important Not to Wait

Delaying treatment allows the infection to spread and increases the chances of needing more complex care later. A neglected infected tooth can lead to:

* Intense, ongoing pain
* Tooth abscesses
* Bone loss in the jaw
* Loss of the tooth altogether
