## Why Dental Crowns Are Used to Restore Teeth

Crowns are custom-made restorations that cover weakened or damaged teeth. They are commonly recommended for teeth affected by large cavities, fractures, severe wear, or root canal treatment.

A crown fits over the damaged tooth and can help:

* Restore chewing ability
* Protect against further damage
* Improve appearance
* Strengthen the weakened structure

With proper placement and maintenance, crowns provide reliable, long-term support.

## Average Lifespan of Different Types of Crowns

The lifespan of a crown depends on the material used and daily care habits.

### Common Crown Materials

* **Porcelain:** Typically lasts 10–15 years.
* **Porcelain-fused-to-metal:** Often lasts 10–15 years or longer.
* **Gold and metal alloys:** Can last 20 years or more.
* **Zirconia:** Known for durability and long-term performance.

Research shows that many dental crowns remain successful for well over a decade when patients maintain healthy oral hygiene habits.

## Factors That Affect Crown Longevity

Several factors influence how long a restoration remains functional.

### Oral Hygiene

Plaque buildup around the crown can cause decay in the underlying tooth.

### Bite Pressure

Grinding and clenching place excess force on restorations, increasing the risk of damage.

### Material Quality

Some materials are more resistant to wear than others.

### Professional Placement

## **Daily Habits That Help Crowns Last Longer**

Small daily habits can go a long way toward keeping your crown in excellent condition.

### Helpful Habits

1. Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste.
2. Floss carefully around the restoration.
3. Use a nightguard if teeth grinding is a concern.
4. Avoid chewing ice and hard objects.
5. Visit your dental office regularly.

### Expert Insight

Many crown failures occur because of problems affecting the natural tooth underneath rather than the restoration itself. Consistent preventive care remains the best defense.

## Signs a Dental Crown May Need Replacement

Even durable restorations eventually wear out.

Watch for:

* Persistent sensitivity
* Visible cracks or chips
* Loose fit
* Discomfort when biting
* Gum recession near the tooth

Early evaluation can prevent the need for more extensive treatment later.

## Common Problems That Can Damage Crowns

Crowns are strong, but they are not indestructible.

Potential causes of damage include:

* Teeth grinding
* Untreated gum disease
* Tooth decay beneath the restoration
* Sports injuries or accidents
* Using teeth to open packages
