## Why Bone Loss Begins After a Tooth Is Lost

Natural tooth roots play an important role beyond holding teeth in place. Every time you chew, gentle pressure travels through the root into the jawbone, helping maintain its strength and density.

When a tooth is lost, that stimulation stops. The body naturally begins breaking down bone in the area through a process called bone remodeling. Bone loss often starts within the first few months after tooth loss and may continue gradually if the missing tooth is not replaced.

The rate of bone loss varies from person to person and may depend on factors such as overall health, gum health, smoking habits, and how long the tooth has been missing.

## How Jawbone Changes Can Affect Oral Health and Facial Structure

Bone loss does not remain limited to the space left by the missing tooth. As the supporting bone changes, it can gradually affect nearby teeth and the way your bite functions.

Over time, bone loss may contribute to:

* Changes in bite alignment
* Movement of neighboring teeth
* Difficulty chewing certain foods
* Reduced support for the lips and cheeks
* A more sunken facial appearance
* Less available bone for future restorative treatment

Because these changes develop slowly, many people do not notice them until they begin affecting comfort, appearance, or chewing function.

## The Unique Role Dental Implants Play in Bone Preservation

While bridges and dentures replace only the crown of a missing tooth, dental implants also include an implant post that functions as the tooth’s artificial root.

The implant is placed within the jawbone, where it gradually bonds with the surrounding bone through a process called **osseointegration**.

Once healing is complete, normal chewing forces are transmitted through the implant into the jawbone, much like a natural tooth root. This stimulation helps maintain bone where the tooth was lost.

Although natural bone remodeling continues throughout life, dental implants are the only commonly used tooth replacement that replaces the tooth root and provides functional stimulation to the jawbone.

Before recommending treatment, your dentist evaluates factors such as bone volume, gum health, medical history, and bite alignment to determine whether implant placement is appropriate.

## Maintaining Dental Implants for Long-Term Success

While an implant cannot develop a cavity, the surrounding gums and supporting bone can still be affected by inflammation if plaque builds up.

Simple daily habits can help protect your implant and the tissues around it:

* Twice-daily brushing with a soft-bristled toothbrush helps remove plaque and supports healthy teeth and gums.
* Clean between your teeth and around the implant every day using dental floss or other cleaning tools recommended by your dentist.
* Keeping up with scheduled dental checkups and professional cleanings helps preserve the health of your implant and the tissues that support it.
* Avoid using tobacco products, as they can interfere with healing and increase the likelihood of implant-related complications.
* Follow your dentist’s personalized home care instructions to support the long-term health and function of your dental implant.

A combination of daily oral care and routine dental visits helps maintain the health, stability, and longevity of dental implants.

## Frequently Asked Questions

[Can bone loss occur after losing just one tooth?](# "Can bone loss occur after losing just one tooth?")

Yes. A single missing tooth can contribute to gradual bone resorption over time, since the jawbone no longer receives the stimulation needed to help maintain its density.

[Is everyone a candidate for dental implants?](# "Is everyone a candidate for dental implants?")

Not always. Your dentist will evaluate your overall health, gum condition, bone volume, and medical history to determine whether implants are appropriate.

[Can bone loss be treated before getting dental implants?](# "Can bone loss be treated before getting dental implants?")

Some patients require bone grafting before implant placement if there is insufficient bone to support the dental implant securely.

[How long do dental implants last?](# "How long do dental implants last?")

With good oral hygiene, routine professional care, and healthy surrounding tissues, dental implants can remain functional for many years.
