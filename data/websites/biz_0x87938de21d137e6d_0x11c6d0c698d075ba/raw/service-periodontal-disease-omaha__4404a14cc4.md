## About Periodontal Gum Disease

Were you aware that over 64,000,000 American adults suffer from periodontal gum disease? Once gingivitis (the first stage of periodontal disease) has advanced, the condition can actually no longer be cured, simply managed. When a patient neglects to properly take care of their smile and oral health, bacteria, plaque and acid begin to build up in the mouth, wreaking havoc on the gums and other supporting structures of the mouth. As the disease progresses, more and more damage is caused, resulting in a variety of symptoms, which include:

* Bad Breath
* Toothache
* Loose Teeth
* Tooth Loss
* Red Gums

* Receding Gums
* Swollen Gums
* Bleeding Gums
* Infected Gums
* Gum Pockets

Many patients, however, report not noticing any symptoms until the condition had progressed past gingivitis. We recommends **[routine dental care](https://andingfamilydental.com/service/routine-dental-care/)** to prevent complications such as gingivitis from ever starting.

Q: What happens if gum disease is left without treatment?
A: Untreated gum disease can progress over time, leading to gum recession, bone loss, loose teeth, and eventually tooth loss. Early diagnosis and treatment can help protect your oral health and prevent more serious complications.

Q: Can gum disease affect my overall health?
A: Research has shown associations between gum disease and certain systemic health conditions, including heart disease, diabetes, and other inflammatory conditions. Maintaining healthy gums may contribute to better overall health.

Q: Does gum disease treatment hurt?
A: Modern gum disease treatments are designed to be as comfortable as possible. Local anesthesia and advanced techniques may be used when necessary to help minimize discomfort during and after treatment.

Q: Is gum disease linked to any other health issue?
A: Yes. Studies have found links between gum disease and several health conditions, including cardiovascular disease, diabetes, respiratory conditions, and complications related to inflammation. Regular periodontal care can support both oral and overall wellness.
