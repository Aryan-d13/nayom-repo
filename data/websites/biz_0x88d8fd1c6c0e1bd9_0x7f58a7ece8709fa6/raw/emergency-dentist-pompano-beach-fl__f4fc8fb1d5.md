## What is Emergency Dentistry?

Emergency dentistry involves treating dental issues that require urgent attention to save a tooth, stop ongoing tissue bleeding, or alleviate severe pain. These situations can arise unexpectedly and necessitate quick action to prevent further complications. Emergency dental services encompass a range of treatments, from pain relief and temporary repairs to more extensive procedures that address the underlying problem.

## What is a Dental Emergency, and What to Do?

Understanding what constitutes a dental emergency and knowing how to respond can make a significant difference in the outcome of your situation. Here are some common dental emergencies and steps to take if they occur:

**Symptoms:** Persistent, severe pain in or around a tooth.

**What to Do:** Rinse your mouth with warm water, gently floss to remove any trapped food, and apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek if there is swelling. Avoid using aspirin on the tooth or gums. Contact our office immediately for an appointment.

**Symptoms:** Visible damage to a tooth, sharp edges, or pain.

**What to Do**: Rinse your mouth with warm water and save broken pieces. Apply a cold compress to the outside of your mouth to reduce swelling, and contact us immediately for an emergency appointment.

**Symptoms**: A tooth completely dislodged from its socket.

**What to Do**: Retrieve the tooth by the crown (avoid touching the root) and rinse it gently with water if it is dirty. Try reinserting the tooth into the socket and holding it by the crown. If you cannot reinsert it, place the tooth in a milk or saline solution container. Seek immediate dental care, as time is crucial for saving the tooth.

**Symptoms**: A missing filling or crown, sensitivity, or pain in the affected tooth.

**What to Do:** Keep the area clean and avoid eating on that side. You can use dental cement at a pharmacy to temporarily cover the area. Contact our office to schedule an urgent visit to replace the filling or crown.

**Symptoms:** A painful, swollen area on the gums, often accompanied by fever and a bad taste in the mouth.

**What to Do:** Rinse your mouth with mild salt water several times daily to reduce pain and draw the pus to the surface. Seek immediate dental treatment to address the infection.
