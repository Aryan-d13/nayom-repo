## **What Are Dental Implants?**

A dental implant is a durable replacement for a natural tooth and functions like one. A dental implant consists of three separate components, which include:

* **Titanium Post:** The titanium post provides a permanent root for the new tooth, surgically implanted directly into the jawbone.
* **Abutment:** The abutment is an attachment that fits on top of the titanium post and serves as a connection between the implant and the visible portion of the new tooth.
* **Crown:** The crown is the portion of the implant that contains the visible part of the new tooth and is shaped to match the patient’s surrounding natural teeth and gum tissue.

Unlike traditional dentures, [dental implants in Austin](https://www.daylightdental.com/services/dental-implants/) fuse to the jawbone and will look, feel, and function like natural teeth once the fusion is complete. Dental implants also help preserve jawbone density, unlike traditional dentures.

## **What Factors Affect the Cost of Dental Implants in Austin?**

There are many variables; each case is unique. The following are a few of the factors influencing the cost:

* **Number of implants —** a higher number of teeth means a higher cost
* **Bone density —** if there is bone volume loss, this must be corrected through a bone graft before placing the implants
* **Type of implant material —** titanium is the standard choice; however, zirconia options may also be available at higher prices.
* **Dentist expertise —** specialists or dental offices focused on placing implants will occupy different price ranges
* **Location** — costs in South Austin or the Domain area may differ slightly from surrounding suburbs
* **Pre-procedure work –** Additional work performed before the implant procedure (e.g., extraction, CT imaging, X-rays) will increase the total cost.

## **Dental Implants vs Dentures — Which Is Better?**

It can be difficult to know which is best for you. Below is a comparison of the two:

* **Dental Implants —** are permanent (last 15-25 years+) and preserve the bone structure; do not need to be removed; can be very low-maintenance
* **Dentures —** have a lower up-front cost than dental implants, but must be replaced every 5-10 years; may come loose and cause discomfort while chewing, and do not prevent bone loss.

Over time, most people find that dental implants are more cost-effective than dentures, particularly because many denture wearers spend additional money on adhesives, relining, and replacement.
