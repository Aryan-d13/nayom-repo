How Do Affordable Dental Implants Work?

The prospect of undergoing dental implant surgery tends to bring up a lot of questions. At its core, a dental implant is a screw-shaped post that is made from hypoallergenic material that we place into your jawbone, which is called an endosteal implant. When there isn't enough bone left in your jaw to support an implant, you could receive a subperiosteal implant that sits above the jawbone line. In either case, the dental implants procedure is meant to help you achieve a replacement tooth that is more stable than removable dentures.

Steps Involved With the Dental Implants Procedure

Some people need bone grafting or treatment for gum disease before we begin the dental implants procedure. Following any pre-surgical care, we'll then prepare the area for the implant by doing a gentle cleaning and using anesthesia to help you stay pain-free. Our dentist then uses their expert touch to insert the implant in your bone or gums where it will then begin the process of fusing to your oral tissue and bone to provide support for the crown.

Taking Care of Your New Implants

When it comes to caring for an implant, you'll find that the steps you'll need to take fit right in with your normal oral hygiene routine. Unlike with dentures, you don't take implants out. Instead, you'll use your toothbrush to clean the crown. Then, you'll floss around the post to remove food debris that gets stuck between your teeth. Then, you'll stick to regular checkups with your dentist to make sure they heal properly and stay in good health for years to come.

What Makes You a Strong Candidate for a Dental Implant?

Determining who needs implants begins with a visit to the dentist. You already may be a good candidate for an implant if you prefer to have a full smile or feel uncomfortable with missing teeth no matter where they are in your mouth. Strong candidates also have good oral health, or they are motivated to improve issues that include gum disease.

How Long Do Implants Last on Average?

There isn't a definite answer to the question of how long does a dental implant last, but we can get pretty close. The average implant lasts about 10 years or more, and the success of your implant mostly depends upon controllable factors that include staying on top of your oral hygiene.
