What Does an Endodontist Do?

Most dental procedures, including teeth cleaning, examinations, crowns and fillings fall into the category of general dentistry. For certain procedures that involve the pulp inside the tooth or the roots of teeth, however, an endodontist is usually needed to achieve the best results.

Endodontists must undergo specialized training after completing their degree in dentistry. This training focuses on the structural elements of the tooth, including the pulp inside the tooth. Nerves, blood vessels and other connective tissues are all found inside the tooth. Endodontists have the advanced training and education necessary to prevent damage to these structures and to perform procedures involving the interior of the tooth and tooth roots.

Root canals are among the most common procedures performed by endodontists on behalf of their patients. If you need a root canal expert in Pinellas Park, chances are you will receive your treatment from dentists who specialize in endodontic procedures.

What Is a Root Canal?

The pulp or interior tissue of the tooth can sometimes become infected or inflamed because of the intrusion of bacteria through holes in the tooth enamel. In these cases, the infection can cause extreme pressure on the nerve endings inside the tooth and can cause severe pain that, in some cases, could prevent patients from managing their daily activities and responsibilities.

During a root canal, your endodontist in Pinellas Park will remove the infected tissue from inside your tooth. It will then be replaced with an inert material that will prevent bacteria from reaching the roots of your teeth and will provide support for the outer structural shell of your tooth. Finally, the tooth will usually be covered with a crown to protect the tooth from further damage.

Working with a qualified root canal specialist in Pinellas Park will allow you to enjoy the best outcomes for these advanced and complicated procedures. Signs That You May Need a Root Canal

Infections and abscesses of your teeth and gums can cause a number of symptoms that can alert you to these issues. Some of these symptoms are listed here:

* Swellings or bumps on the gums can be a sign that an infection is building up in the roots of a tooth. This can also be accompanied by an unpleasant taste in your mouth or a feeling of pressure when you touch the swollen areas.
* Pain that centers on a particular tooth or on a specific area of your mouth can also be a sign of an abscessed or infected tooth. For some patients, the pain that accompanies these dental issues can be overwhelming and can limit their ability to perform their normal activities and tasks.
* Discolored teeth or redness of the gums can also be a warning sign that an infection or inflammation is present in your teeth.
